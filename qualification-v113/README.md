# V113 — battery power-path necessary-condition screen

## Decision and scope

Do not adopt the $9.49 USE-18650-2600PCBJST or either flat protected candidate for the present phone power budget. The 246524 pack remains an unresolved engineering lead, not an approved selection. `battery_necessary_power.py` provides 50 reproducible necessary-condition cases that separate a provably insufficient **sustained hypothetical load allocation** from a case that merely is not ruled out. It does not prove a pack's pulse performance or establish the duration or duty cycle of a real call.

The chosen sensitivity case is the existing design allocation of 3.8 V × 2 A = 7.6 W sustained modem output, plus a 3.3 V × 0.1 A host at assumed 80% conversion efficiency (0.4125 W input), and optionally a 5.3 V × 0.131 A display/backlight allocation at assumed 75% efficiency (0.9257 W input). These loads and efficiencies are **engineering assumptions**, not measured bounds or manufacturer guarantees. The actual waveform may differ. They are not simultaneously guaranteed minimum loads of the device. The LTE-only operating mode and exact variant are still unverified; GSM remains a hard gate.

The 2.6 A case is an optimistic **pack-only** comparison, not a rated connected path. The [pack specification](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/5335/USE-18650-2600PCBJST.pdf) identifies a JST-PH-2P connector and AWG24 leads, while the [JST PH series specifications](https://www.jst-mfg.com/product/pdf/jpn/PH.pdf) list 2 A AC/DC with AWG24. The exact contact, housing and mating header have not been confirmed. If the actual assembly is the standard JST PH configuration and its 2 A limit applies, the complete path must be screened at no more than that rating; do not infer any higher-current approval from the pack or PCM. At an illustrative 4.2 V terminal and 2 A, only 7.062 W remains after the assumed 1.338 W auxiliary allocation, so the 7.6 W sustained modem allocation fails even ideally. The model includes this as a separate, conditional PH-path case; it is not a pulse rating or a measured waveform.

At a hypothetical **loaded pack terminal voltage** of 3.3 V, with the light allocation on, the 2.6 A continuous limit of the USE-18650-2600PCBJST offers 8.58 W at the terminals; after the *assumed* 1.338 W auxiliary input allocation, 7.242 W remains, less than the assumed 7.6 W modem output even with an ideal modem converter and no wiring loss. For a sustained interval with those conditions, the budget is impossible; this is not a claim that the modem ever draws 2 A continuously or that a brief pulse exceeds its permissible rating. At 3.6 V the same modeled case would require at least 94.74% modem-converter efficiency before any wiring loss; no guaranteed converter efficiency under that operating corner is established.

With the same illustrative 3.3 V terminal voltage and auxiliary allocation, using **3.6 A** as an engineering ceiling below the lower end of the Jauch 246524 datasheet's 4–10 A overcurrent detection range yields a necessary 72.09% efficiency. That is a mathematical lower bound, not a guaranteed efficiency, verified PCM hold current, protected-pack limit, operating cutoff, or approval. The data sheet does not supply a maximum pack DC pulse resistance over temperature, state of charge and age. It describes 1 C maximum discharge and 2 C only for 2–3 ms; it does not establish immunity to an arbitrarily long 3.6 A pulse. Jauch says its sheet is generally descriptive rather than a guarantee, so obtain current supplier confirmation.

**Revision and lot gate:** the [18 August 2025 Jauch sheet](https://www.jauch.com/downloadfile/68a40e0c799795c25355af4f8dbddf6ad/jauch_db_lp735977jh.pdf) lists 4–10 A PCM overcurrent detection and a 2 C pulse for at most 2–3 ms; the manufacturer's still-hosted [October 2022 Rev 1.3 sheet](https://www.jauch.com/downloadfile/677e3e4f04fa96c9cb9cbc715e565a615/matd_246524_20200507_4800mah_-_lp735977jh_1s1p_2_wire_70mm.pdf) lists 5–10 A and no corresponding pulse rating. Neither sheet establishes which limits apply to a particular shipped lot. A 2 C number based on 4.8 Ah must not be treated as a guaranteed 9.6 A protected-pack output. The 2025 sheet lists a 10 kΩ B3435 NTC but shows only red and black leads, so external access to that sensor is unconfirmed. Ask the manufacturer for the actual pack revision, protection delay/repetition data, DC pulse-drop curves and sensor wiring before qualification.

## Keep modem requirements separate

The archived EG912U-GL QuecOpen V1.0 section 3.5.2 recommends at least 2 A supply capability for LTE-only and 3 A for GSM. The same reviewed document has separate BB/RF pin comments and Table 41 current figures; these must not be blindly added or converted to a continuous 2 A duty cycle. An archived text extraction of Table 41 lists IVBAT typical 1.7 A / maximum 2.5 A and a maximum burst drop of 400 mV. Its visual transcription and applicability to the selected AT-command SKU and current hardware revision remain unverified; confirm the figure, operating conditions and rail measurement point. The 400 mV burst-drop requirement is separate from remaining above the modem's [3.3 V lower supply limit](https://developer.quectel.com/en/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2024/11/Quectel_EG912U-GL_LTE_Standard_Specification_V1.2.pdf). Neither datum establishes pulse duration, frequency, temperature, or per-mode envelope. The [TI TPS63802 data sheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps63802.pdf) gives a 2 A output example at 3.3 V output, not a guarantee of 2 A at the proposed 3.8 V output across all input, transient and thermal corners.

## How to use the model

`V × I_limit >= P_aux + P_modem / efficiency` is a necessary energy balance at the stated loaded battery terminal voltage, assuming USB contributes no power, constant output power, no stored-energy contribution and zero harness/charger losses. `required_efficiency()` reports the lowest theoretical modem-converter efficiency or flags a strict energy deficit. Equality needs an ideal lossless converter and is explicitly not labeled impossible. A result below 100% is only **not ruled out**: voltage droop, converter peak/thermal limits, PCM trip timing, regulator stability, capacitor characteristics and protection coordination still need evidence. This is not a transient simulation. The five terminal-voltage points are conditional examples, not measured battery voltages or a proposed undervoltage threshold. The code does not approve a pack.

Run `python3 qualification-v113/battery_necessary_power.py` and `python3 -m unittest discover -s qualification-v113 -p 'test_*.py' -v`. The eight independent tests cover the failure example, algebraic inverse, ideal boundary, monotonicity and invalid inputs. The generated `necessary-power-results.json` records all 50 cases. No hardware, PCB, supplier configuration or firmware was changed.

## External evidence still required

1. Confirm intended cellular country/carrier and exact certified modem SKU, hardware revision and voice profile; obtain its authoritative current waveform over all permitted modes and reset/fallback states.
2. Reconcile the actual USE pack connector manufacturer, contacts, housing, mating header and full-assembly current and temperature ratings; the PCM threshold is not a normal operating rating. For each pack finalist obtain manufacturer-confirmed minimum/maximum PCM trip current **and time curves**, connector and wire limits, usable SOC and temperature range, maximum aged/cold full-pack **DC pulse drop** including PCM, leads and contacts, swelling and temperature-sensor access. Do not infer DC bounds from 1 kHz impedance.
3. With the actual current waveform and end-of-life envelope, calculate and measure loaded voltage at pack, converter and each modem rail, verify both rail floor and allowed burst droop, and test brownout/restart/charging interactions, capacitor effective values, full layout and thermal response.
4. Continue mechanical mating, C93151 import quarantine, assembly source rights, and factory process checks. No sample, purchase or fabrication has been authorized by this analysis.
